# Overview

Strengthening your core isn't just about getting "six-pack abs"—it’s about building a solid foundation for your spine and improving your overall functional movement. A strong core helps with everything from better posture to preventing lower back pain.Here is a breakdown of effective core exercises categorized by their primary focus.

1. Stability & Isometric Holds
These exercises focus on resisting movement and keeping the spine neutral.

* Forearm Plank: The gold standard. Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels, engaging your glutes and drawing your navel toward your spine.
* Side Plank: Focuses on the obliques and hip stability. Stack your feet or drop a knee for a modification.
* Dead Bug: Lie on your back with arms up and knees at a 90-degree angle. Slowly lower the opposite arm and leg toward the floor while keeping your lower back pressed firmly against the ground.

2. Dynamic Strength

These moves involve controlled movement to build muscle endurance.

* Bird-Dog: From all fours, extend the opposite arm and leg simultaneously. This is excellent for spinal alignment and the lower back.
* Hollow Body Hold/Rock: A gymnastics staple. Lie on your back and lift your legs and shoulders slightly off the ground, shaping your body like a banana.
* Mountain Climbers: From a high plank position, drive your knees toward your chest. This adds a cardiovascular element while challenging core stability.

3. Rotational & Oblique Focus

These exercises help with twisting movements and side-to-side stability.

* Russian Twists: Sit with your knees bent and feet slightly off the ground. Rotate your torso from side to side. (Tip: Move your shoulders, not just your arms).
* Bicycle Crunches: Focus on bringing the opposite elbow to the opposite knee in a slow, controlled "pedaling" motion.
* Pallof Press: If you have access to a cable machine or resistance band, pull the band to your chest and press it straight out in front of you, resisting the pull to the side.


A Pro-Tip for Results
The secret to core training isn't the number of reps; it's breath control. Try to exhale deeply during the hardest part of the movement (the "exertion"). This engages the transverse abdominis, the deepest layer of your core that acts like a natural corset.

# 10 minute circuit

This 10-minute circuit is designed to be performed in a **"45/15" format**: 45 seconds of work followed by 15 seconds of rest. This gives you just enough time to transition to the next move while keeping your heart rate up.

No equipment is needed—just a bit of floor space.

---

## The "Solid Foundation" 10-Minute Routine

Complete this circuit **twice**. Move slowly and focus on "quality over quantity."

| Order | Exercise | Key Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **01** | **Dead Bug** | Keep your lower back glued to the floor. |
| **02** | **Bird-Dog** | Pause for 2 seconds at the top of each extension. |
| **03** | **Forearm Plank** | Don't let your hips sag or pike up. |
| **04** | **Bicycle Crunches** | Slow and controlled; think "shoulder to knee." |
| **05** | **Side Plank** | 22 seconds on the left, 22 seconds on the right. |

---

### Tips for Success
* **The 15-Second Transition:** Use this time to get into the starting position for the next exercise. Take a couple of deep breaths.
* **Don't Hold Your Breath:** It’s tempting during planks! Try to breathe through your nose to keep your nervous system calm.
* **Modify as Needed:** If your lower back starts to arch during the Dead Bug or Bicycle Crunches, don't lower your legs as far.

### How to Progress
Once this feels "easy," you can increase the intensity by:
1.  Reducing the rest time to 10 seconds.
2.  Adding a light weight (like a water bottle) to the Russian Twists or Dead Bugs.
3.  Increasing the work time to 50 seconds.

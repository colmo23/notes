To get the most out of these movements (and keep your knees happy), focus on **control** rather than speed. Here is a step-by-step breakdown of how to perform each exercise in the "Power Base" circuit with perfect form:

---

### 1. Bodyweight Squats
* **The Setup:** Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward (about 15°).
* **The Move:** Imagine sitting back into an invisible chair. Lower your hips until your thighs are parallel to the floor (or as low as you can go with a flat back).
* **Key Tip:** Keep your weight in your **heels and mid-foot**. If your heels lift off the ground, you're leaning too far forward. Drive through your heels to stand back up and squeeze your glutes at the top.

### 2. Reverse Lunges
* **The Setup:** Stand tall with feet hip-width apart.
* **The Move:** Take a large step **backward** with one foot. Lower your back knee until it almost touches the floor. Both knees should form roughly $90^\circ$ angles.
* **Key Tip:** Keep your torso upright. Stepping backward is generally "kinder" to the kneecaps than stepping forward because it keeps the front shin more vertical.

### 3. Glute Bridges
* **The Setup:** Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart. Your heels should be close enough to your glutes that you can almost touch them with your fingertips.
* **The Move:** Squeeze your glutes and lift your hips toward the ceiling until your body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees.
* **Key Tip:** Pause at the top for one second. **Do not arch your lower back** at the top; the lift should come from your hips, not your spine.

### 4. Lateral (Side) Lunges
* **The Setup:** Stand with feet together.
* **The Move:** Take a wide step out to the right. Keep your left leg perfectly straight while bending your right knee and sitting your hips back. Push off the right foot to return to center. Switch sides.
* **Key Tip:** Keep both feet pointing forward. This exercise is great for "opening up" the hips and working muscles that we often neglect in daily life.

### 5. Calf Raises
* **The Setup:** Stand near a wall or chair for balance if needed. Feet should be hip-width apart.
* **The Move:** Rise up onto the balls of your feet as high as possible, then slowly lower back down until your heels just graze the floor.
* **Key Tip:** Imagine a string pulling the top of your head toward the ceiling. To make it harder, do these on the edge of a step so your heels can drop below the level of your toes.

---

### A Quick Check on "Mind-Muscle Connection"
When doing lower body work, it's easy for the quads (front of thighs) to take over everything. If you want to target your glutes specifically:
1.  **Drive through the heels.**
2.  **Squeeze the muscle** you are trying to work at the peak of the movement.
3.  **Slow down the "down" phase**—this is where the real strength is built.

### Key Form Tips for Home Workouts
* **Squats:** Keep your chest up like you're reading a book on the wall in front of you. Sit back into your heels, not your toes.
* **Reverse Lunges:** Stepping *backward* is often gentler on the knees than stepping forward. Aim for 90-degree angles with both legs.
* **Glute Bridges:** Lie on your back, feet flat. Squeeze your glutes hard at the top of the movement. To make it harder, try doing one leg at a time!
* **Lateral Lunges:** Keep your stationary leg straight while the "stepping" leg bends. This is great for hip mobility.

### Why this works with Core Training
Think of your body like a bridge. Your **core** is the suspension cables, but your **legs and glutes** are the massive concrete pillars. If the pillars are weak, the cables have to work twice as hard. Doing these two circuits on alternating days is a fantastic way to build a functional, pain-free body.
